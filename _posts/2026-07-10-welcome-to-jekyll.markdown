Digital spaces are weird.

I can chat with friends at any time of the day, without having to look presentable, or go anywhere or plan anything. I don't need to greet them or say goodbye. I can socialise with groups of people that I know intimately, and laugh at our shared jokes, even though none of them know me. I can see what it's like to bikepack from Alaska to Argentina; I can hear an Andean farmer wish me safe travels.

I feel real joy from all of this; there is no other kind. So why, in the longer term, does it make me so sad?

I've also come to the conclusion than I don't really want to know. Knowledge is not always good: sometimes knowledge can turn something meaningful into something sad. In this case, I think knowing what real life has, and what digital spaces correspondingly lack, would be dangerous - like explaining a joke, something would be lost in the process of understanding.

The important part is this: digital spaces are useful for building connections with real people who you otherwise would never get to meet or know. That's the power of the internet. But they should only *augment* the connections you build in the real world, and never replace them.
