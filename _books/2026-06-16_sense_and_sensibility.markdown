> Elinor, who had now been for some time reflecting on the propriety or impropriety of speedily hazarding her narration, without feeling at all nearer decision than at first, heard this; and perceiving that <b>as reflection did nothing, resolution must do all</b>, soon found herself leading to the fact.

I initially gave this 4 stars, thinking that I didn’t feel invested in the husband of either Elinor or Marianne.

Two days later, I upped it to 5 stars. This book is not a romance, which after reading Emma, P&P, Persuasion, and Northanger, I struggled to appreciate. Sense and Sensibility is about finding the resolution to continue when you have no power to advance. It’s a message that resonated with me incredibly strongly.

I believe the marriages at the end are not supposed to be romantic. At the very least, Elinor’s isn’t - it’s a shock ending, a twist, that everything turns out okay despite all her efforts, not because of them.
