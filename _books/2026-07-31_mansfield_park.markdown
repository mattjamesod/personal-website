> We all have a better guide in ourselves, if we would attend to it, than any other person can be.

Mansfield Park is not what I expected. It begins like every other Austen novel: there are 1-3 sisters, and they must be wed. One of them marries well, and the others serve as varying cautionary tales about what happens to women who Fate does not take kindly to.

Except the weddings are done by page 3. And the sister who marries well turns out to be spoilt and indolent, and her family are all varying degrees of horrible - and our protagonist is a decided outsider, from the wrong branch of the family, and therefore undeserving of kindness.

It's a trope to say Mansfield Park "isn't my favourite Austen novel, but...". Instead I'll say Mansfield Park left me with more questions than any of Austen's other work. It raises more questions than it answers - and they are good questions, that I will be pondering for a long time. In some sense, I think this is Austen's most "worthy" novel.
