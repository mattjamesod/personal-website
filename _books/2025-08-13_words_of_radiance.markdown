<div>This book isn't (literally) perfect, which makes it an unusual inclusion on my 5-star list. What separates 4 and 5 stars for me is a feeling that I wouldn't know where to begin improving it.<br><br>That isn't the case with Words of Radiance: I think the middle of Shallan's part 2 plot is a bit of a drag, and the number of characters she becomes friendly with from Kaladin's WoK plot strains my credulity a bit. Sadeas' viewpoints are a bit bland. Some of the humour doesn't land.<br><br>In a 1000 page novel though, these small faults get swept away by the storm. Shallan is the best she is in the entire series, and the themes of the Lightweaver order that pervade the whole book are engaging and beautiful: how do you know truth? Who it is you want to be? How do we become better without feeling like we need to be fixed?<br><br>Kaladin's plot speaks to the frustration of those questions more than Shallan's does, and their meeting in this book, after separate plots in WoK, is a fantastic payoff of the scale promises made by the series. The chasm scene where the two properly get acquainted is possibly the character peak of the entire published series at time of writing, despite promising a romance that never comes (but that's on Oathbringer, not WoR).<br><br>Aside from all that lofty nonsense, the book is just consistently awesome, which is a requirement for a 5-star epic fantasy. Every part delivers icon moments. And no Sanderson climax has ever topped "<em>Stretch forth thy hand!</em>"<br><br><br></div>
