He does not consider that colonial societies are mainly occupied by wealthy colonisers (likely to be literate) and their slaves (who are not considered people for the purpose of measuring literacy rates).<br><br>Most of the book is taken up with arguments of this kind for why 18th and 19th century America is the greatest place to ever exist, setting the stage for his denunciation of the 20th century version.<br><br>Chapters 6 and 7 are where the actual value of the book lies, and the Cold War aestheticism is minimised.
